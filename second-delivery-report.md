# The Wonderful Wizard of Oz  Knowledge graph evaluation
Second delivery - 27 November 2026

A python script evaluated four measurements of the graph:

**Coverage.** Coverage measures how many queries return any results. All six queries returned at least one row, giving us **6/6 coverage**. It should be pointed out that this measurement does not prove whether the returned results are correct or complete. As such, they must still be compared with the requirements of the competence questions, and the graph's source of truth. After this analysis, we determined all of the answers were correct. For example, the graph identified Aunt Em and Uncle Henry as Dorothy’s relatives in Kansas and linked the Witch of the East to her silver shoes. 

**Precision.** Precision measures the proportion of triples from the sample that, after manual verification, were deemed correct. The script randomly selected 20 of the graph’s 51 data triples. In our worksheet, we marked all 20 as correct, giving the sample a **precision of 100% (20/20)**. The sample included details from both chapters, from Toto living in Kansas to the Witch of the East owning the silver shoes. Since the remaining 31 triples fall outside this check, it does not prove they are correct. As such, they should still be checked on the book. However, the 100% precision of the sample gives us some confidence in the correctness.  

**Vocabulary reuse.** Vocabulary reuse measures the proportion of distinct domain predicates and classes which originate from external vocabularies, instead of being created in the project's namespace. Of the 27 distinct domain predicates and classes we used, **12 came from existing vocabularies (44.4%)**. Seven came from Schema.org, three from Dublin Core Terms and two from FOAF. RDF, RDFS, OWL and XSD are excluded from the count. Existing terms worked well for details such the book's author and character's occupations. We used local terms for details such as which characters appear in each chapter. We also recorded four external identifier links separately. Kansas has two (WikiData and DBPedia), and Dorothy and L. Frank Baum have one each (both from WikiData).

**Orphan nodes.** Orphan nodes are nodes from local data that do not participate in any relationship between resources. For this measurement, only relationships between resources, excluding types, labels and literal values are considered. We found **0 orphan nodes among the 18 local data nodes**. Every data node can therefore be reached from another resource or followed to one. It's important to note that this does not mean the graph forms a single connected component. As always, the existence of these relationships is not a guarantee of correctness, and every relationship should still be verified in the book.
